Aim & Learning Objectives

Experiment Aim

To study and observe position-based rearrangement (permutations) in classical cryptography using the Rail Fence Cipher (keyless transposition) and the Columnar Transposition Cipher (keyed transposition), and to demonstrate how inverse permutations restore the original plaintext during decryption.

Learning Objectives

  • Understand the distinction between Substitution (altering symbol identity) and Transposition (altering symbol position).
  • Observe the zig-zag rail traversal and its cycle period: T = 2(r - 1).
  • Learn how keyword alphabetical ranking dictates column extraction order.
  • Analyze handling of irregular (ragged) matrices without filler symbols.
  • Observe the forward permutation pi(i) and inverse pi^-1(j).

Transposition ciphers preserve letter frequencies while destroying digram/trigram patterns. They provide the diffusion component used in modern block ciphers like DES (P-Boxes) and AES (ShiftRows).

Theory

1. Transposition Ciphers & Permutation Theory

In a transposition cipher, message symbols are unchanged but their positions are permuted. For a message of length n, encryption is a bijective mapping:

pi : {0, 1, ..., n-1} → {0, 1, ..., n-1}   such that   C[pi(i)] = P[i]

Decryption uses the inverse permutation pi^-1:

P[i] = C[pi(i)] ⇔ P[pi^-1(j)] = C[j]

2. Rail Fence Cipher (Keyless)

Characters are written in a zig-zag pattern across r horizontal rails. The ciphertext is produced by reading rows from top to bottom.

Cycle Period: One full oscillation spans 2(r - 1) characters.
Row Assignment: Row for index i oscillates between 0 and r-1.

Example: HELLOWORLD, 3 Rows

Row 1: H . . . O . . . L .  (Indices 0, 4, 8)
Row 2: . E . L . W . R . D  (Indices 1, 3, 5, 7, 9)
Row 3: . . L . . . O . . .  (Indices 2, 6)
Ciphertext: H O L E L W R D L O

3. Columnar Transposition (Keyed)

Plaintext is written row-by-row into a grid beneath a keyword of length m. Columns are read vertically in alphabetical order of the keyword letters.

Key Ranking: ZEBRA → A(1), B(2), E(3), R(4), Z(5).
Duplicate Rule: Repeated letters (e.g. BALLOON) are ranked left to right.

Ragged Columns

When n is not a multiple of m, the first n mod m columns have ceil(n/m) rows and the rest have one fewer. Decryption must account for these unequal heights.

4. Security Properties

Since transposition does not substitute characters, single-letter frequencies match natural language. However, digram and trigram patterns are disrupted. Vulnerabilities include:

  • Brute Force: For Rail Fence with small row counts, trying values 2–10 quickly reveals plaintext.
  • Anagramming: Sliding columns to find common digrams reconstructs the permutation without the keyword.

Procedure

Rail Fence

Encryption

  1. Clean plaintext: remove spaces/symbols, convert to uppercase.
  2. Initialize r empty row buffers.
  3. Walk characters diagonally down (row 0 to r-1) then up (r-1 to 0), repeating.
  4. Concatenate rows top-to-bottom to produce ciphertext.

Decryption

  1. Compute the zig-zag coordinate trail for length n.
  2. Count how many characters belong to each row.
  3. Slice ciphertext into row-sized segments.
  4. Re-traverse the zig-zag path, pulling from each row buffer in order.

Columnar Transposition

Encryption

  1. Clean plaintext and keyword. Let key length = m.
  2. Rank keyword letters alphabetically (ties: left to right).
  3. Write plaintext row-by-row into a ceil(n/m) x m grid.
  4. Read columns top-to-bottom in rank order.

Decryption

  1. Compute each column's height (full vs. short columns).
  2. Allocate ciphertext slices to columns in rank order.
  3. Read the reconstructed grid row-by-row.
